# Cast of Nammude Kochi

Every talkable person with a stable explicit id: the same 130 ids and the same placements as Mumbai (`docs/CAST.md`) and Chennai (`docs/CHENNAI_CAST.md`), reskinned for Kochi. This file is the single source for the 130 Malayalam conversations: the id, who the person is, where they stand, what they look like and what they are doing. Locals speak **Malayalam** (`ml`); the five foreign tourists speak **English** (`en`). Role keys in code are unchanged (for example `chaiwalaUncle` is now the chaya kada owner Kuttan Chettan, `cricketKid` is now a football kid), only the look, name and conversation changed.

Source of truth for the looks: `src/worlds/kerala/people/castLooks.js` (named overrides by id), `people/roles.js` + `roles2.js` (role defaults) and `people/variation.js` (seeded citizens). Exact spots: `window.__kerala.npcs.npcs` (dev) or `src/worlds/kerala/population4.js` and `details.js`. Non-talkable background: the stray dog at the chaya kada, crows and pigeons, and the passengers painted in the metro windows (the women's coach shows only women).

## The hero: Ananya

**Ananya**, 24, a backpacker from Jaipur travelling through Kochi and the backwaters, learning Malayalam on the road. NPCs call her "mole" (an affectionate word for a girl), "chechi" (from the kids) or "backpacker-mole" and suggest places to see (the Chinese nets, the island chapel, a kettuvallam, a metro ride), correcting her Malayalam kindly. She is clearly an adult, as tall as the local adults: a long black plait under a wide straw hat with a red band, freckles, a loose oatmeal cotton shirt with the sleeves down over cropped olive cargo pants (bare ankles), leather sandals, a big rust-orange rucksack with a blue foam sleeping mat rolled and strapped on top and a water bottle at the side, and a folded paper map in her right hand. No NPC wears that combination: the other tourists carry a camera and a small backpack and wear caps; nobody else has the rucksack-with-mat, the straw hat or the map (`variation.js` `notAnanya` strips them from every generated person).

## What Kochi people wear (checked against real photos, see the report)

- Men: the white **mundu** (cotton cloth tucked at the waist, with a thin gold, brown or black border), worn full length by elders and priests and **folded up to the knee** by vendors, porters, fishermen and boatmen, with half-sleeve or long-sleeve shirts (white, cream, stripes, black for the film fan) and a white **thorth** (thin towel) on the shoulder; checked **lungis** for stall men and teashop workers; jeans and T-shirts for the young; office wear (long-sleeve shirt, trousers, ID lanyard, teal near the metro) around Infopark and the metro; **khaki** for auto drivers, bus conductors, police and checkpost guards; the Kerala Police uniform is khaki shirt and trousers with a brown leather belt, black shoes, a khaki peaked cap and a lathi; red shirts for porters; red or orange T-shirt and bag for delivery riders; the temple priest is **bare-chested with the sacred thread**, a white mundu with a gold border and sandal paste on the forehead; the Mollywood fan wears a black shirt with a white mundu folded up.
- Women: the cream **set-saree** (set-mundu) with a gold **kasavu** border and a cream or coloured blouse (very common, from grandmothers to office-goers), other sarees, churidar or short kurta with jeans, office wear with a lanyard; jasmine (mulla) in the hair on some (temples, festivals) but fewer bindis than in Chennai; **nuns in white habits** near the island chapel (respectful: gentle, never a joke); umbrellas are black.
- Kids: shirts and shorts, churidar tops, school whites and navy, and **football jerseys** (sky-blue and white stripes, yellow) because Kerala loves football.

## Vehicles and train

Autos are **black with a yellow upper cowl and trim** under a black canvas hood (about one in four has a yellow hood); KSRTC buses come in the classic red lower body with a cream upper, and in white with blue stripes; the **private buses** are sky blue with white bands and a yellow stripe and carry a big painted owner's name over the windscreen and on the sides (invented names: Anjali, Tharangam, Kayal, Maithri, Arunima, Sneha); call taxis are white sedans; plates read `KL 07 ..` (Ernakulam). The Kochi Metro is teal with a dark glass band, a black-masked cab front and a pale roof, lettered മെട്രോ / METRO; the **women's coach** has a pink stripe, pink door panels and the lettering സ്ത്രീകൾ / WOMEN, and only women are drawn in its windows (the other coaches say പൊതു / GENERAL).

## Sites used below

Chaya kada square (the spawn square with Kuttan Chettan's chaya kada, the puttu and appam cart, news stand, banana and fruit stall, jasmine stall, the Onam pookalam, the Malayalam film poster wall, the football patch); the Kerala tiled houses ch1 to ch3 and the flats (verandahs, street tap); the Kerala temple (copper-roof shrine with the brass deepastambham, flower stall, chappal stand, bell); the Kochi Metro station platform; Fort Kochi by the Chinese nets (quay, boats, ferry and water-metro booth, photo tripod, bus stop, auto stand, junction); Mattancherry (Dutch) Palace; Ernakulam Junction forecourt; Aspinwall House with the giant Kathakali mask; the St. Francis Church book stall; the Jew Town synagogue clock tower ledge; the Infopark (gate booth, cab line); the Marine Drive walkway (benches, backwater, boats); Bolgatty island (the palace, the island chapel, candle and flower stalls, the kulukki sarbath stall); Cherai beach (pazham pori stall, corn and coconut carts, pony ride, sand art); the Goshree bridge checkpost. Props that stand in for a Mumbai one: the Elephanta ferry booth is the water-metro ticket booth, the tiffin pile is the delivery scooters, the horse ride is the Cherai pony ride, the Ganpati poster is the Onam pookalam poster, the Haji Ali juice centre is the kulukki sarbath stall, the chadar stalls are candle and flower stalls, the pav bhaji stall is the pazham pori stall, the stock ticker is the "Now hiring" board at Infopark. The 20 multi-step roles (the ones mapped to the 20 Mumbai multi-step roles) are marked **(multi-step)**; the other 110 get one short conversation each. For the dialogue writer: banter is warm and a little teasing, everyday spoken Malayalam with a little English mixed in ("bus stop", "ticket", "office", "metro"), and the chayakkada, the bus and the beach all have regulars with strong opinions.
