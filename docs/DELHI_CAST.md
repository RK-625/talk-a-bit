# Cast of Apni Dilli

Every talkable person with a stable explicit id: the same 132 ids and the same placements as Mumbai (`docs/CAST.md`), Bengaluru (`docs/BENGALURU_CAST.md`) and Hyderabad (`docs/HYDERABAD_CAST.md`), reskinned for Delhi NCR. This file is the single source for the 132 Hindi conversations: the id, who the person is, where they stand, what they look like and what they are doing. All locals speak **Delhi Hindi** (`hi`: "bhaiya", "haan ji", "scene kya hai", "chal na", "koi na", "pakka", "tension mat le", a light Punjabi tinge such as "paaji", "oye" and "chak de"; easy and polite, no slurs); the five foreign tourists speak **English** (`en`). Role keys in code are unchanged (for example `chaiwalaUncle` is now the chai-stall owner Bittu Bhaiya and `priest` is now the granthi); only the look, the name and the conversation changed. Where a role has no explicit override in `castLooks.js`, the row describes the role's own look in `people/roles.js` / `roles2.js` (seeded details such as shirt colour may vary a little).

Source of truth for the looks: `src/worlds/delhi/people/castLooks.js` (named overrides by id), `people/roles.js` + `roles2.js` (role defaults) and `people/variation.js` (seeded citizens: salwar-kameez with a dupatta, a few sarees, kurta-pajama on men, jeans + T-shirts or jackets and sweaters, office wear with lanyards, about 9% of adult men in a plain turban with a full beard). Exact spots: `window.__delhi.npcs.npcs` (dev) or `src/worlds/delhi/population4.js` and `details.js`. Non-talkable background: the stray dog at the chai stall, kites, crows and pigeons at India Gate, and the passengers painted in the Delhi Metro windows (the first coach shows only women).

## The hero: Rahul

**Rahul**, 23, a UPSC aspirant from Patna, in Delhi for civil-services coaching. NPCs may call him "Rahul", "beta", "bhai" or "bhaiya", ask about his exam and the coaching, and offer chai and advice. He is clearly an adult, a little taller than the locals: short messy black hair, **round glasses**, a **mustard-turmeric kurta** worn over **dark-indigo jeans**, brown sandals, a wristwatch, a **thick stack of five books pinned under his left arm** (red, blue, yellow, green and purple spines with gold title bands) and a **khaki cloth jhola** slung over the right shoulder. No lanyard, no laptop backpack, no resume folder, no check shirt (those belong to the other worlds' heroes). No NPC wears the book stack, the khaki jhola or the mustard-kurta-over-indigo-jeans pair (`variation.js` `notRahul`).

## What Delhi people wear

- Women: **salwar-kameez with a dupatta** (printed, bright, a dupatta over the shoulder or, at the gurudwara, drawn over the head), a few sarees on older women and stall holders, jeans with a kurti or a jacket, office wear near Cyber Hub; bindi on some, a red ribbon on a plait, **no jasmine in the hair** (that was Hyderabad).
- Men: **kurta-pajama** (pale kurta, white pyjama), jeans with T-shirts, shirts and **jackets / sweaters** (the Delhi winter haze), office wear with a lanyard near Cyber Hub, the Metro and Connaught Place; khaki for autos, DTC conductors and guards; a white shirt, navy trousers and a white helmet-style cap for the traffic police; red shirts for the Old Delhi station porters; red T-shirts and a plain red bag for delivery riders; white Gandhi caps on some elders.
- **Sikh men** in plain turbans (orange, navy, white, sky-blue, cream) with a full beard, shown respectfully and without caricature: the granthi, the sevadar, the shoe-keeper, a cab driver, the horse handler, an elderly metro passenger and a man out for a chat. At the gurudwara everyone is barefoot and covers their head.
- Kids: shirts and shorts, salwar tops, school whites and navy.
